Nanoparticle Delivery for Antibiotics to Prevent Citrus Greening

Percentage of extracted Vanadium in each plant organ after being applied on leaves.

Presenting at Meeting of the Minds poster session.

Project Summary

Starting in December of 2022, I started working in the Environmental Engineering Lab at Carnegie Mellon University with Dr. Greg Lowry and Dr. Hagai Kohay. I was working on a project aiming to effectively deliver antibiotics to citrus plants using nanoparticles. The first step in my project was to determine how accurately we could measure concentrations of tracer metals in different plant organs using a digestion procedure and ICP-MS analysis. I applied a Gadolinium solution to the citrus tree's leaves, roots, and trunk, and then digested each organ and recorded the concentration that was measured by the ICP-MS instrument. The results of this step are shown in the poster linked below.

After this initial testing, we recieved nanoparticles containing Vanadium from Purdue University and did a size comparison of the nanoparticles with and without a surfactant added. Once this was completed, I performed an analysis on how the nanoparticles moved through the plant when applied to the leaves of a citrus plant. I applied a solution of the nanoparticles to the leaves of the plant, left them for roughly a week, and then digested each organ. The yield was 90% and the results of where the Vanadium was found is shown in the graph above.

I presented my work at a poster session in the Civil and Environmental Engineering Department at the end of summer 2023, and then again at the Meeting of the Minds symposium in spring 2024.
