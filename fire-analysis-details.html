Project Summary

In the final project for Applied Statistical Modeling for Environmental Management, my teammates and I aimed to investigate the relationship between wildfires, AQI, and social vulnerability in California. We had three main research questions:

  1. Is the total wildfire burned area associated with the annual mean AQI at the county level after controlling for temperature, drought conditions, and percent urban land cover?
  2. In Los Angeles County specifically, is the burned area associated with an increase in the number of days with an AQI above 100 the threshold marking conditions unhealthy for sensitive groups?
  3. Is the wildfire-AQI association moderated by county-level social vulnerability?

In our analyses we found that wildfire-burned areas is a significant driver of higher AQI across California. In Los Angeles, more burned area leads to more unhealthy air quality days (AQI > 100). And finally, social vulnerability is linked to higher baseline AQI but does not change the wildfire–AQI relationship.
